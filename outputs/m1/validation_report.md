**For M2:**

- Use the A2 target (target_in_effect) as the outcome, never the F17 0-year point.
- F17 forwards are forwards of the actual cash rate, so while it sat below the target they sit below the target path even with perfect foresight. With error = outcome - forecast this adds a positive mean error; on M2 forecast dates (last F17 day of each month) the average is +8 bp in 2020, +7 bp in 2021, +4 bp in 2022, +3 bp in 2023, +2 bp in 2024, +1 bp in 2025. It works against the term premium, which pushes the error negative. A forecast that uses only date-t data removes it: forward(t, h) - (0-year(t) - target_in_effect(t)). Whether to report it next to the raw forward is an M2 decision.
- Outcome date: the forward at h/12 years is for the night t + 365.25 h / 12 days, but the outcome used is target_in_effect(t + h calendar months). The two dates fall on opposite sides of a decision for: t = 30 Jun 2026, h = 3: 30 Sep 2026 -> 4.60, 29 Sep 2026 -> 4.35. M2 should pick one rule and show these cases both ways.

## 9. What the F17 forward rates are - PASS

**What the RBA says.**

- RBA Bulletin, March 2012, Finlay and Olivan, 'Extracting Information from Financial Market Instruments' (the method behind F17), https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/bulletin/2012/mar/6.html. It defines the zero-coupon yield from the discount curve as z(t) = -log(d(t)) / t, which is continuous compounding (the page does not use the words 'continuously compounded'; the log formula implies it), and the forward curve as f(t) = z(t) + t z'(t), which is the instantaneous forward. In words, the forward curve is "the interest rate agreed today for overnight borrowing at a date in the future". It also says "The forward and yield curves start at the prevailing cash rate."
- RBA RDP 2008-09, Appendix A, https://www.rba.gov.au/publications/rdp/2008/2008-09/appendix-a.html (the fitting method): inputs are OIS rates "for maturities extending up to 1 year" and bond yields "for maturities greater than or equal to 18 months"; the fit ensures "the 1-day yield is given by the overnight cash rate"; at 1 year the average absolute error against OIS is about 3.5 bp and the largest 12 bp. Whether today's F17 (built from F16 inputs since Dec 2023) still uses OIS at the short end is not verified.
- F17 table notes (Notes sheet of https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/xls/f17hist.xlsx): a web search summary reports that they call the yield and forward curves continuously compounded and say no adjustment is made for risk premia. That file is not on disk and was not downloaded, so this wording is not verified here.
- RBA, Changes to Statistical Tables, https://www.rba.gov.au/statistics/tables/changes-to-tables.html: on 29 Dec 2023 "all series in F17 will be replaced with series calculated using the new series" of table F16 (a change of inputs); on 31 Mar 2023 F17 data before 1 Jan 2021 were deleted for commercial reasons; on 8 Aug 2016 F17 was revised for a data correction and a change in method. The note does not say whether past dates were recalculated. This file starts in 2017, which suggests the history was republished from the new inputs; if so, the split at 29 Dec 2023 below compares date ranges, not methods. Either way F17 is a revised dataset, not what was published at the time, but each day's curve is fitted to that day's prices only, so no later information leaks into a forecast made from it.

**Conclusion.** The F17 forward rate at maturity m is the instantaneous (overnight) forward rate for a date m years ahead, continuously compounded, from the same curve as the yields. For M2, the forecast of the cash rate h months ahead is the F17 forward at maturity h/12 years (0.25, 0.5, 1.0) read directly, with no conversion: the cash rate is a simple overnight rate, and continuous and simple overnight rates differ by about r^2 / (2 x 365), at most 0.029 bp at the sample's highest 0-year rate (4.60%).

Reading the short end (RDP 2008-09, App. A, if the method still applies): the 3 and 6-month forwards come from the OIS-fitted segment, so they are close to OIS-implied forwards of the actual cash rate, and the M5 'OIS benchmark next to F17' would largely repeat them. The 12-month forward sits at the end of the OIS inputs, next to the 1-year to 18-month stretch with no inputs, where the paper reports the largest fitting errors; treat 12-month results as less reliable. ## 10. Yield curve control (YCC) - FLAG

Dates and levels are from RBA, Review of the Yield Target, https://www.rba.gov.au/monetary-policy/reviews/yield-target/index.html: a target of around 0.25 per cent for the 3-year AGS yield introduced on 19 Mar 2020, lowered to around 0.10 per cent on 3 Nov 2020, discontinued on 2 Nov 2021; the target bond was the April 2023 bond, then the April 2024 bond from 21 Oct 2020. There was no 0.50 per cent yield target: 0.50 was the cash rate target until the cut that took effect on 20 Mar 2020.

Timing: a YCC announcement on day d applies from the F17 close on d (the same rule as target_known_at_close: 2.30 pm release, 4:30 pm close). The 19 Mar 2020 package was off-cycle; its media release (2020-08, https://www.rba.gov.au/media-releases/2020/mr-20-08.html) announced a press conference at 4.00 pm AEDT that day, so it came out before the close, and said bond purchases would "commence tomorrow". The data agree: the 3-year zero went from 0.50 to 0.31 on the close of 19 Mar 2020, before the cash rate cut took effect on 20 Mar.
